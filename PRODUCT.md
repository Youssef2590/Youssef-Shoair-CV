# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Hiring managers, tech leads, and engineering directors at mid-to-large companies. They're scanning portfolios on a laptop, often tabbing between candidates. They need to feel, within 3 seconds, that this person builds things at a higher level than the average applicant. Secondary audience: fellow engineers who appreciate craft.

## Product Purpose
Personal portfolio for Youssef Shoair, a software engineer specializing in AI integration, agentic workflows, and scalable frontend architecture. The site itself IS the proof of skill. It must demonstrate mastery of advanced frontend techniques (GSAP, scroll-driven choreography, layout complexity, typographic confidence) while remaining fast and accessible. Success = the visitor thinks "this person clearly knows what they're doing."

## Brand Personality
Precise, inventive, confident. Not corporate. Not playful. The vibe is a senior engineer's workbench: every tool has a place, every detail is deliberate, but the work itself is ambitious and surprising.

## Anti-references
- Generic dark-mode developer portfolios with coral accent and card grids
- Template-feeling hero (big name, one-liner, scroll button)
- SaaS-landing-page aesthetics (gradient blobs, glass cards, hero metric grids)
- Bland timeline experiences with identical dots
- "Creative developer" sites that sacrifice usability for spectacle

## Design Principles
1. **Practice what you preach** — The portfolio demonstrates the skills it claims. Complex UI techniques are used because they prove capability, not for decoration.
2. **Earn every element** — Nothing decorative without function. If a motion exists, it aids comprehension or creates narrative.
3. **Controlled tension** — Mix precision with surprise. Rigid grids next to unexpected type. Calm pacing next to dense information.
4. **Show the range** — Each section should feel like a different demonstration: layout mastery, typographic confidence, interaction design, data presentation.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG AA minimum. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. All interactive elements keyboard-accessible. Sufficient contrast on all text.
